# BACKGROUND

First to Fight—Our Nation’s Expeditionary Force in Readiness � The Marine Corps is a globally responsive,

lethal, and resilient combined-arms naval expeditionary force that projects power from sea to land and land to

sea, fighting as a Marine Air-Ground Task Force across all domains � Sustaining this vision requires accelerated

modernization to ensure Marines are fully trained, organized, and equipped for the rapidly evolving challenges

of future warfare �

The phrase  “First to Fight”  has embodied the warfighting ethos and expeditionary spirit of the Marine Corps

for generations � It has deep roots within our history and culture – highlighting our dedication to the mission,

to readiness, and to responding rapidly in the defense of the nation and its people � First to Fight , A Capstone

Operating Concept reinforces our enduring commitment to serve as the premier expeditionary force in-readiness

of the United States, prepared to defeat our nation’s adversaries in any clime and place �

Adapting to changing threats is nothing new for the Marine Corps � Much like how the coastal defense battalions

defended advanced naval bases from maritime and aerial threats in WWII, today we have reconstituted the

capability to project power from land to sea � First to Fight  is based on the simple idea that Marines will defeat

adversaries by combining shore-based sea denial with offensive amphibious operations � The future operating

environment requires Marines to maneuver as effectively in and through cyberspace, space, and the electromagnetic

spectrum (EMS) as they do in the air, on land, and at sea �

Combined arms now means all-domain, with sensors, weapons, command and control, and the systems that

connect them enabled by and dependent on cyber, space, and electromagnetic capabilities � In this reality, data

is a military resource and fighting for information is as important as physical objectives � Protecting our networks

and information from enemy collection and exploitation is critical to the success of military operations � Innovating

with emerging technology is essential and will enable the Marine Corps to rapidly modernize �

# CENTRAL IDEA: THE HYPOTHESIS

First to Fight: Our Nation’s Expeditionary Force in Readiness. The Marine Corps is a globally responsive,

lethal, and resilient combined-arms naval expeditionary force that projects power from sea to land and land

to sea, fighting as a Marine Air-Ground Task Force across all domains in contested environments to deter,

deny, and defeat adversaries. Marines conduct sea denial, contribute to sea control, and conduct amphibious

operations to deny adversary freedom of action, while extending Joint Force commanders’ operational reach.

Modernized forward forces, optimized to operate in the littorals, seize and hold key maritime terrain to deliver

lethal effects, sense and shape the operating environment, and close kill webs in support of fleet maneuver

and joint campaigns.

# STAND-IN FORCE OPERATIONS

Marine forces, maneuver and operate within the contested littorals, exploiting them as a singular and integrated

battlespace � The Marine Corps is a naval expeditionary force uniquely capable of transitioning ready-to-fight

combat forces across domain boundaries in the littorals – into the sea, onto land, and through the air � Amphibious

Ready Groups / Marine Expeditionary Units (ARG/MEUs) and Marine Littoral Regiments (MLRs) maneuver in

the littorals using both organic and supporting capabilities 3� These units evade enemy sensors and weapons to

project power from key maritime terrain that is hard to reach by other elements of the Joint Force �

Marines conduct stand-in force operations that help deny the seas to adversaries � Marines can conduct these

operations from anywhere, but they are particularly well-suited to operating in high-threat areas because they

are survivable: hard to detect and engage � By operating in forward locations with long-range strike capabilities,

Marines deny enemies the ability to use the seas for their own purposes, while also gathering critical information

about enemy forces and disrupting their ability to operate � This forward, survivable, lethal presence is a key

component of deterrence and denial �

Marines conduct intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance activities to gather information about enemy

targets and conduct operations to generate intelligence and counter adversary deception � With unique access

as a stand-in force, Marines use advanced sensors and communications systems to aggregate and disseminate

targeting information to the joint force, allies and partners at every step of the targeting cycle �

Marines in challenging forward locations also use counter-reconnaissance; military deception; and other tactics,

techniques, and procedures to disrupt the enemy’s ability to understand the environment and target friendly

forces � By interfering with enemy sensors and systems, and feeding them false information, Marines create

confusion and uncertainty among the enemy, making it harder for them to launch effective attacks �

Through their forward presence and ability to conduct sea denial operations, Marines help to secure maritime

chokepoints � This capability is critical to supporting fleet operations � By operating in forward locations and working

closely with allies and partners, the Marine Corps demonstrates its commitment to regional security and its ability

to respond quickly to emerging threats, deterring enemies and protecting the United States and its interests �

# SEIZE, SECURE, HOLD OR OCCUPY KEY MARITIME TERRAIN

The Marine Corps executes offensive operations in the littorals to conduct sea denial and ensure freedom

of movement for friendly forces � By defeating enemy units on land, Marines help establish sea control and

prevent enemies from blocking the movement of friendly forces � Through amphibious operations, Marines

can maneuver over long distances in contested waters to secure key maritime terrain, giving friendly forces an

asymmetric advantage �

Offensive amphibious operations and stand-in force operations are powerful complementary tools that allow the

Marine Corps to achieve a wide range of missions � By launching attacks from the sea and sustaining operations

over long distances, Marine landing forces can overcome enemy defenses and defeat their forces on land �

Marines can also occupy friendly territory that reinforces allies and support sea control over key littoral areas �

This flexibility and responsiveness enable Marine forces to work with other forward forces and allies to achieve

their objectives �

Marine landing forces are designed to seize, secure, hold or occupy terrain to improve their military position and

defeat adversaries � Marines can influence adversary decisions and deny them control of key areas and will often

do so working with allies and partners � Through exercises and training with partner forces, Marines demonstrate

their commitment to defending their interests and practice cooperation in preparation for future operations � By

doing so, Marines can prevent the enemy from taking control of these areas without a fight – and deny a fait

accompli by our adversaries �

# CRISIS RESPONSE OPERATIONS

Marines play a vital role in responding to crises that threaten American lives and interests � Their mission is to

safeguard American lives, reduce risk, and provide leaders with the time, information, and options they need to

make strategic decisions � Marines must be ready to respond quickly in unexpected situations, and be prepared

to fight if necessary �

Marines support the Joint Force Commander in contested areas like the First Island Chain with crisis response

operations such as raids, seizure of key maritime terrain, and long range strikes which provide key contributions

to deterrence strategies � By securing key maritime terrain, Marines enable the Joint Force Commander’s decision-

making while complicating the enemy �

Amphibious forces are particularly well-suited for crisis response, as they can quickly launch operations from the

sea and respond to changing situations � ARG/MEUs are designed to rapidly respond to crises, prioritizing their

equipment and capabilities to focus on fighting for access to critical areas quickly � They work together with other

forces, including special operations forces, to create a powerful and effective response force � By being the first

to respond, Marines either resolve the issue or set the stage for a broader joint force response �

The Marine Corps must always be ready to respond, adapt, and win � By being agile, adaptable, and prepared,

Marines can make a difference in the most critical situations, protecting American lives and interests and advancing

the nation’s objectives �

b� Conduct Littoral Maneuver.  Forces that can fight effectively across multiple physical domains have an

advantage over those limited to a single domain � By designing forces that can maneuver within and across

domain seams, the Marine Corps can exploit gaps in adversary anti-access and area denial systems, which

are often focused on defending individual domains rather than the spaces between them � As a naval

expeditionary force, the Marine Corps must be able to move effectively from sea to land and land to sea,

c� Conduct All-Domain Combined Arms.  To generate effects in all domains, the Marine Corps will require

robust, cutting-edge capability sets enabled by AI, as well as flexible command arrangements that can

synchronize all-domain combined arms fires with cross-domain maneuver � Combined arms must now be

all-domain, incorporating effects in and from cyberspace, space, as well as the electromagnetic spectrum

in conjunction with kinetic fires to achieve a unified and cohesive operational approach � This requires

accelerating the fielding of ground and aviation capabilities including long-range anti-ship missiles, small

unmanned aerial systems, precision fires, and modernized electronic warfare � Make no mistake, all-domain

combined arms effects remain centered on lethality �

d� Adopt a “Hard to Find – Hard to Kill” Approach.  To survive inside a contested environment, future Marine

forces must employ a “hard to find—hard to kill” approach � This approach involves two key components:

winning the counter-reconnaissance battle and making it difficult for adversaries to target friendly forces � The

first component, “hard to find,” requires understanding the adversary’s collection methods and defeating

them through a combination of active and passive measures, including maintaining a light footprint, using

mobility and maneuver, practicing signature management, and employing deception � The second component,

“hard to kill,” involves understanding the adversary’s targeting processes and disrupting that process through

movement, dispersion, and sensor defeat � Distributing forces and assets can further complicate the adversary’s

ability to allocate collection and targeting resources, ultimately enhancing Marines’ survivability in contested

areas � Additionally, investments in the broader ground based air defense portfolio, counter-unmanned aerial

systems, and integrated air and missile defense systems enhance and distribute overall force protection �

e� Enhance Distribution and Sustainment Operations.  The Marine Corps must prioritize the timely and

reliable sustainment of forces in challenging environments � The tempo of military operations depends on

the ability to sustain and support forces, particularly in areas the enemy seeks to disrupt or control � As the

Marine Corps operates in increasingly contested and complex environments, its supply lines and logistics

systems are vulnerable to disruption, making it essential to create a network of flexible, redundant, and

resilient supply chains � Actions can be taken now that can contribute to this effort: prepositioning critical

equipment and supplies, leveraging local contractors and host-nation support, integrating into joint and

naval logistics networks, increasing the variety and quantity of distribution platforms, implementing advanced

manufacturing, prioritizing demand reduction during requirements generation, and using data science and

advanced analytics to anticipate requirements � By taking a proactive and adaptive approach to logistics

prior to armed conflict, the Marine Corps can enhance its ability to operate effectively, withstand enemy

pressure, and enable protracted wartime operations in a contested or denied environment �

f� Accelerate Decision Making in Maneuver Warfare.  The Marine Corps’ warfighting philosophy of maneuver

on-board processors, and AI will help commanders quickly orient on the situation and decide on appropriate

l� Harness Data as a Military Resource.  In the past, control of commodities like coal and oil was a key military
